## What Is a Rotary Damper?

A **rotary damper** is a compact motion-control component that creates controlled resistance during rotational movement. It is used when a lid, cover, handle, tray, storage box or small mechanism needs to open, close or return more smoothly.

Without damping, a mechanism may spring open too quickly, drop suddenly, hit an end stop, or create an unpleasant plastic impact sound. A rotary damper slows the motion and makes the movement feel quieter, smoother and more intentional.

For product designers, this small component can make a clear difference in perceived quality. A controlled opening action, a soft closing feel and lower operating noise all help the final product feel more refined.

## Why Rotary Dampers Are Used

Rotary dampers are widely used in mechanical structures where the user directly feels the movement. The goal is not only to slow a part down. The goal is to create a stable, repeatable motion that improves comfort, reduces impact noise and protects surrounding plastic parts.

Typical benefits include:

- Smoother opening and closing movement.
- Less impact noise at the end of travel.
- Better perceived product quality.
- Lower stress on hinges, covers and small plastic structures.
- More consistent movement across repeated use.
- A premium soft-motion feel without motors or electronics.

Because rotary dampers are compact and passive, they are especially useful in small mechanisms where there is limited space and no need for electronic control.

## How a Rotary Damper Works

The core principle of a rotary damper is the viscous resistance of the damping grease inside the damper body. When the output shaft or toothed interface rotates, the internal shaft, base and damping grease work together to create resistance. This resistance converts sudden movement into controlled rotational torque.

In general, higher damping grease viscosity creates higher torque and a heavier motion feel. Lower viscosity creates lighter torque and a freer motion feel. The final torque is also affected by the internal contact area, flow path, seal design, working angle and available internal volume.

The physical size of the damper also matters. A smaller rotary damper is easier to fit into tight spaces, but it has less room for damping grease and less working surface area. A larger damper can usually support higher torque because the internal structure can create more viscous resistance.

## Rotary Damper Torque Selection

Selecting the right rotary damper is not only about the weight of the lid or cover. The correct torque depends on the movement path, center of gravity, opening angle, target speed, transmission ratio, available space, operating temperature and assembly tolerance.

If torque is too low, the mechanism may still move too quickly and create impact noise. If torque is too high, the movement may feel sticky, slow or difficult to open. For production projects, TEAO usually recommends testing the damper in the real assembly and fine-tuning the torque during the sample stage.

Useful information for rotary damper selection includes:

- Target torque and acceptable tolerance.
- Rotation direction and working angle.
- Available installation space.
- Cover or mechanism weight and center of gravity.
- Target opening or closing speed.
- Operating temperature range.
- Cycle-life requirement.
- Annual production volume.

## Temperature Performance: -40°C to 110°C

Rotary damper performance is strongly affected by temperature. In cold conditions, grease viscosity increases and torque normally becomes higher. In hot conditions, grease viscosity decreases and torque may drop.

TEAO rotary dampers can be designed for operating environments from **-40°C to 110°C**. At **-40°C**, TEAO typically controls torque increase to about **60% to 80%**. This helps avoid an overly heavy feel in cold environments.

At high temperature, TEAO can usually control torque attenuation within **20%**, helping the mechanism maintain a more consistent movement feel in hot vehicle or appliance conditions.

## Cycle Life and Reliability

TEAO rotary dampers can support **200,000 to 300,000 cycles** depending on the model, torque setting and application conditions. After long-term cycling, some torque attenuation is normal, but TEAO can typically control attenuation within **10% after 100,000 cycles** in validated conditions.

TEAO evaluates common failure modes during design and validation, including abnormal operating noise, shaft breakage, output-interface cracking, leakage, unstable torque and abnormal wear. This helps the final damper meet demanding automotive and high-end product requirements.

## Design Notes Before Using a Rotary Damper

Before selecting a rotary damper, confirm the available installation space, rotation direction, target torque, working angle, temperature range, life-cycle target and output engagement method. Incorrect installation, excessive side load or poor alignment can create noise, shorten life or cause leakage.
